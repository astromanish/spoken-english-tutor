# Demo rehearsal — Job interview, every line verified live

Companion to `video-script-4min.md`. Every "what comes back" below is the **actual
output of this app**, not a guess. Run through it once out loud before recording.

**The one rule:** the interviewer's questions follow *your answer*, so no two takes ask
the same thing. Do not memorise his questions. Memorise **your** lines — each one is
self-contained and works after whatever question he just asked. The question column is
only there so you recognise the shape when you hear it.

## The five beats (in this order, ~90 s)

### 1 — Confidence gate · say `"Three."`

He answers something like *"Great. Can you tell me about your previous work experience?"*
and the level badge on screen changes.

**What it proves:** the confidence answer sets the correction level; he does not correct it.

### 2 — A clean answer · no correction invented

> **"I worked as a machine learning engineer at StatusNeo for two years, building
> recommendation models."**

Comes back as a reaction + a follow-up, e.g.:
*"That sounds like solid experience. What specific tools or programming languages did you
use most often in those projects?"*

**What it proves:** he does not invent mistakes to look busy. Four clean answers in a row
produced zero corrections. Say this on camera — it is the trust beat.

### 3 — The money shot · present perfect

> **"I am working in this company since 2019."**

Exact output:
> `"I have been working in this company since 2019."` — *"am working" ki jagah "have been
> working" bolna chahiye, kyunki 2019 se ab tak ka time hai.*
> → *"What specific achievements are you most proud of during that time?"*

**What it proves:** the two-part correction — English for what to say, Hinglish for why —
followed by the next question in the same turn. This is the frame you zoom on.

### 4 — Stuck in Hindi · the empathy beat

> **"Mujhe samajh nahi aaya, main kya bolun?"**

Exact output:
> *Tension mat lo, main aasan kar deta hoon.* `"Can you tell me about your last job?"`
> → *"What did you enjoy most about that work?"*

**What it proves:** Hindi in, reassurance + a sentence he can copy, in Hinglish, out. No
correction, no shame. For a video about people *scared* of English, this is the strongest
30 seconds you have — do not cut it for time.

### 5 — Barge-in · talk over him

Start answering, let him begin the reply, then talk over him after his first few words.
The pill flips *Speaking → Listening to you* and his voice stops mid-sentence.

**What it proves:** turn-taking is real, not push-to-talk.

## Extra ladder (if you want 30 more seconds of proof)

Each line was verified to produce a correction. Use one, not all — three corrections in a
row turns a conversation into a lesson.

| Say | Correction you get |
|---|---|
| "I has two years experience in machine learning." | `"I had two years of experience in machine learning."` + *'I has' ki jagah 'I had' bolna chahiye* |
| "I did my graduation on 2022." | `"I graduated in 2022."` |
| "I is good in python and I am do coding daily." (say it after answering "Two.") | `"I am good at Python, and I do coding daily."` + *'good in' ki jagah 'good at'* |

## If something goes wrong on camera

| Happens | Do this |
|---|---|
| He answers a mistake with no correction | Say the level-2 backup: **"I am working in this company since 2019."** It fires every time. Do not explain it yourself on camera. |
| He asks something you did not plan for | Answer with whichever line above still fits — they are not tied to the question. Worst case, the "stuck in Hindi" line resets the beat cleanly. |
| A correction goes past too fast to read | Cut and re-ask; do not slow the video down in the edit. |
| The whole turn comes back in one blob without the Hinglish line | Re-record that turn. It is rare, and it is the shot you cannot fake. |

## What the questions actually look like (from a real run)

Not a script — the shape. Yours will differ, and that is fine:

1. *"Can you tell me about your previous work experience?"*
2. *"What specific tools or programming languages did you use most often?"*
3. *"How would you explain that project to someone who isn't technical?"*
4. *"Can you share an example where that patience led to a successful outcome?"*
5. *"Which part of the deployment process interests you the most?"*

Notice what he does: he reacts to what you actually said, then asks about **that**. He
never dumps a fixed list of interview questions. If a take feels "off-script", it is
working.
